# Constraints Interview Guide

Goal: find the one or two things that actually limit how fast The O-Ring Store can grow, using first principles and Theory of Constraints. Everything else in the four plans (inventory, sales, marketing, administration) should be built to serve that constraint.

Run it a module at a time. Each module is about 15 to 20 minutes. Ask one question at a time, follow up with "why?" when something matters, and bring baseline numbers in when they fit (marked **Data:**).

Every module ends the same way: "Of everything we just talked about, what's the one thing that, if it were fixed tomorrow, would grow sales the most?" Log the answer as a constraint hypothesis.

---

## Module 0: The Goals Themselves

Purpose: understand what the goals really are before solving for them. First principles starts with the goal.

1. When you say $10 million this year, what does hitting it give you? What changes for you, the crew, the family?
2. Where did $10M, $20M, $50M come from? Gut, a plan, a conversation, something you read?
3. **Data:** 2026 is tracking about $4.8M (up about 9% year to date). Getting to $10M means roughly doubling. How do you think about that gap? Is the $10M a target, a stretch, or a "north star"?
4. What would $20M look like on a normal Tuesday? How many people, how many orders, what building, what kind of customers?
5. If we hit $20M but margin dropped from 60% to 35%, would that be a win? What matters more, sales, gross margin dollars, or cash you can take out?
6. How do the personal goals tie in (vacations every three months, the land and the water, the Airbnbs, energy independence)? Which of those need the business to grow, and which need it to run without you?
7. Inversion: what would guarantee we never get to $10M?

## Module 1: Demand (Market Constraint)

Purpose: is the limit that not enough customers find us or choose us?

1. Where do new customers come from today? Rank them: website, Google, Amazon or marketplaces, word of mouth, reps, McMaster-type resale, trade shows, something else.
2. **Data:** About 15,500 customers bought in the last 12 months, and about 14,700 of them spent under $1,000. Average invoice is around $175. Is that the business you want to be, the business you are by accident, or both?
3. **Data:** No single customer is more than about 2% of sales. Top customers include Cabka, McMaster-Carr, Acorn Engineering, Depot Street, Semco Tank. What do the big ones have in common? How did we win them?
4. When someone calls and we can't help them, what usually happened? Didn't have it, price, lead time, didn't make it? Do we track those lost orders anywhere?
5. If we had twice the inventory tomorrow, would sales double? Why or why not? (Key question for the "capital is the constraint" belief.)
6. If we had twice the website traffic tomorrow, would sales double? What would break first?
7. Who's the customer we're best at serving that we don't have enough of? Industry, size, how they buy.
8. Who are we losing to, and why? McMaster, Global O-Ring, Marco, Apple Rubber, Parker distributors, Amazon?
9. What do we do that nobody else bothers to do? (Circle of competence.)

## Module 2: Sales Process and Capacity

Purpose: is the limit how many quotes, calls, and accounts the team can handle?

1. Walk me through how a typical quote goes, from the first email to the order. Who touches it, how long does it take?
2. How many quotes go out a week? What share turn into orders? What happens to the rest?
3. **Data:** HubSpot shows about 184 open deals worth about $269K, 110 won and 108 lost in the last year (about a 50% win rate by count), average deal around $1,200. Does HubSpot reflect real life, or does most business never touch it?
4. If Daro had a clone tomorrow, what would the clone do first? Would it bring in more sales, or just handle the same sales faster?
5. Is anyone doing outbound selling, actually going after named accounts? If not, why not?
6. What does a great salesperson at TORS do differently from an average one?
7. How are people paid? What are they rewarded for? (Incentives model.)
8. Which accounts could buy 5 to 10x what they buy now? What's stopping that?

## Module 3: Inventory and Supply

Purpose: is the limit what we can stock and how fast we can get it?

1. How do we decide what to stock, how much, and when to reorder? Who decides? Is it a system or a gut call?
2. **Data:** About $1.43M of inventory on hand across about 12,500 SKUs. Gross margin runs about 60%, so turns are about 1.4 a year and GMROI is about 2.0. About $250K (3,250 SKUs) hasn't sold a single unit in 12 months. What's your reaction to those numbers?
3. Why do we carry the slow stuff? Is it insurance for good customers, bought for a one-time order, a vendor minimum, or just never cleaned up?
4. How often do we lose a sale because we're out of stock? Do we know, or is it a feeling?
5. Who are the top 5 vendors? Lead times, minimums, payment terms? Any of them a single point of failure?
6. Where does our own brand fit (Olypsys® and other private label)? Margin vs. buy-resell?
7. If you had another $1M for inventory tomorrow, exactly what would you buy? Can we list it?
8. What would happen if we sold or returned that $250K of dead stock and put the money into the fastest movers?

## Module 4: Capital and Cash

Purpose: test the core belief that capital is the constraint. First principles: capital for what, and what does it return?

1. When you say capital is holding us back, what's the first thing you'd do with more of it? The second? The third?
2. Has there been a moment in the last year where we had to say no or wait because there wasn't cash? What was it?
3. What does the cash cycle look like? How fast do customers pay vs. how fast we pay vendors? **Data:** Open AR is about $170K, roughly 12 days of sales, with only about $7K more than 30 days past due. Customers pay fast.
4. What financing do we have today: line of credit, SBA, equipment loans, vendor terms? What's been offered and turned down?
5. How do you feel about debt? About outside investors? About selling part of the business? (Policy constraints often live here.)
6. **Data:** Rough math: at 60% margin and 1.4 turns, every extra $1M in sales needs about $285K more inventory. At 3 turns it's about $135K. Does it change the picture if we can grow by turning stock faster instead of by borrowing?
7. **Data:** Q3 2026 NetSuite shows sales of about $1.23M, cost of goods about $491K, and operating expense about $325K. That expense number looks light for 17 people. Is payroll or anything else booked outside NetSuite? (We need a true profit picture before we talk about funding.)
8. How much cash do you want held back as a cushion no matter what? (Margin of safety.)

## Module 5: Operations and People

Purpose: is the limit the building, the process, or the people?

1. Walk the building with me. Receiving, stock, picking, packing, shipping, custom work. Where do things pile up? Where do people wait?
2. **Data:** About 2,200 invoices a month, roughly 100 a day. What's the most we could ship in a day with the current crew and building? What breaks first?
3. Which jobs, if that person called in sick for two weeks, would hurt the most? (Single points of failure.)
4. How much of Marty's week is work only Marty can do? How much is work someone else could do with training or a system?
5. Same question for Daro and Darion.
6. What tasks eat the most hours and add the least value? (Automation and AI targets.)
7. 13,000 square feet: how full is it? At what sales level do we run out of room?
8. ISO 9001:2015: is it a sales weapon, a cost of doing business, or both? Does it slow anything down?

## Module 6: Administration, Succession, and Policy

Purpose: find the rules and habits that quietly cap growth, and make sure the business can run without Marty.

1. What are the "we don't do that" rules? Products, customers, industries, pricing, terms, channels. Where did each rule come from?
2. What decisions have to go through you today? Which of those could Daro or Darion make with a clear guideline?
3. Inversion: you're out for six months starting Monday. What goes wrong first? Second? What's written down vs. in your head?
4. What's the plan if something happens to you? Ownership, banking relationships, vendor relationships, key customers.
5. What do Daro and Darion each want the business to become? Does it match your picture?
6. What meetings, reports, or numbers do you look at every week? Which ones actually change a decision?
7. Groundwork Brewing: how much of your time and capital does it take? Does it compete with TORS for either?

## Module 7: Marketing

Purpose: is the limit how many of the right people know we exist?

1. What's working in marketing right now? How do we know?
2. What's the website's job? How much of the business comes through it? Is it the front door or a catalog?
3. When an engineer searches for an O-Ring size or a material, how often do we show up? Do we know?
4. **Data:** About 22,700 contacts were loaded into HubSpot in July 2026 and about 1,350 more since. What are we doing with that list? Email? Nothing yet?
5. What content do we have that nobody else bothers to make? (Size charts, material guides, compound data sheets, Olypsys®.)
6. If marketing were the only thing we fixed this year, what would you want it to do?

## Module 8: Synthesis

Purpose: land on the constraint and agree on it.

1. Read back the constraint hypotheses, ranked, with the evidence for each. Ask Marty to re-rank.
2. Test the top one with the doubling question: "If this one thing doubled, would the whole business grow? If anything else doubled and this didn't, would we grow?"
3. Run the Five Focusing Steps on it together: exploit (free fixes now), subordinate (what everyone else does differently), elevate (what to spend on).
4. Pre-mortem: it's October 2027 and we missed. Why?
5. Re-look at the goals with the math. Agree on what the next 12 months actually targets.
6. Write `strategy/constraints-interview/findings.md` and map what each of the four plans has to do.

## Follow-up toolkit

Use these when an answer needs digging:

- **Why?** (up to five times)
- "How do we know that?" (Known, Believed, or Guess?)
- "What would have to be true for the opposite to be right?"
- "What does it cost us when that happens? Ballpark dollars?"
- "And then what?" (second-order)
- "If we just stopped doing that, what would happen?"
- "Who else should I ask?" (Daro, Darion, the crew may see it differently. Note it.)
